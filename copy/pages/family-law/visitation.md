Some parents call because they need a fair schedule in the first place. A divorce or separation is underway, and nobody has said yet when the children will be with whom.

## If your visits are being denied

It is natural to want to push back hard. The steps below usually serve you better.

- **Keep following the order yourself.** Show up on time, at the place and hour the plan sets.
- **Write down every missed visit.** Note the date, the time and what the other parent said.
- **Save the messages.** Texts, emails and voicemails about the schedule are worth keeping.
- **Don't take matters into your own hands.** Talk to a lawyer before you hold back support or keep the child past your time.
- **Call before it becomes a pattern.** A petition for enforcement is the court's tool for this. {fact:F227}

## How the court sets a schedule

Decisions about parenting plans are almost always made based on the best interests of the children. {fact:F270} So it helps to frame your request around your child's needs and your place in your child's life, rather than the other parent's faults.

## Questions about visitation

### What does "primary residential parent" mean?
It is the parent the child lives with. {fact:F269} The other parent's time is set out in the visitation schedule. {fact:F222}

### The other parent keeps cancelling my visits. What can I do?
If you are being denied visitation, you can file a petition for enforcement with the court. {fact:F227} Keep a written record of each missed visit to bring to your consultation.

### Can the visitation schedule be changed?
Yes. A parenting plan can be modified when circumstances change, such as a move or a change in either parent's finances. {fact:F223} See [parenting plan modifications](/family-law/parenting-plan-modifications/).

### Is visitation the same as custody?
They are related but not the same. Custody covers decision-making and where the child lives; visitation is the schedule of time with each parent. {fact:F312,F222} See [child custody](/family-law/child-custody/).
