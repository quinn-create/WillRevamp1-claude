## A plan is not set in stone

Children grow. Jobs change. Parents move, or simply stop seeing eye to eye. A schedule built for a toddler may not fit a teenager.

A parenting plan can be modified to account for changes in the parents' or the children's circumstances. {fact:F223} Will handles these cases, starting with the filing for modification of the existing plan. {fact:F205}

## Common reasons to revisit a plan

The old plan may need to change when one of these happens. {fact:F223}

### A parent is moving
When one parent plans on moving away, the current schedule may no longer work. {fact:F223} The plan may need to catch up with the new distance.

### Money has changed
A change in either parent's financial situation, such as a new job or a lost one, can be a reason to revisit the plan. {fact:F223} Child support is one of the terms a parenting plan can include. {fact:F222}

### You no longer agree
Sometimes terms that once worked no longer do. When one parent, or both, no longer agree with the existing terms, a modification is one way forward. {fact:F223}

## Asking for a change, or answering one

**If you want the change,** Will can propose it and take it through the legal process. {fact:F221,F205}

**If your former spouse wants it and you don't,** Will can help you work out what to do next. {fact:F221}

Either way, decisions about parenting plans are almost always made based on the best interests of the children. {fact:F270} So expect the focus to be on how the change affects the children, not only on whether a parent agrees with it. {fact:F270}

## What to bring to your consultation

- **Your current parenting plan** and any court order that goes with it.
- **A short timeline** of what has changed, and when.
- **Papers that show the change**, such as a new lease, a job offer or pay records.
- **Your child's current schedule**: school, activities and care.
- **Any written proposal** the other parent has sent you.

## Questions about changing a parenting plan

### Can a parenting plan be changed after the divorce is final?
Yes. A parenting plan can be modified to account for changes in the family's circumstances. {fact:F223} Will handles requests for modification of an existing plan. {fact:F205}

### What are common reasons for a change?
A parent planning to move, a change in either parent's finances, or parents who no longer agree with the existing terms. {fact:F223}

### My ex wants to change our plan, and I don't agree. What now?
Will can help you figure out what to do next. {fact:F221} Expect the focus to be on the children, since parenting-plan decisions are almost always made based on their best interests. {fact:F270}

### Do both parents have to agree to a change?
Not necessarily. If your former spouse proposes a change you don't agree with, Will can help you respond, and a disputed change can be argued before the court. {fact:F221,F313}
