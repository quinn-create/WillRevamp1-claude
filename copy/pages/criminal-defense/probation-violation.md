## You were almost through it

Probation often means the end of a case is in sight. A violation accusation puts that at risk, and it can feel like starting over.

It isn't. An accusation is not a finding. Maybe you missed something for a real reason. Maybe you did nothing wrong at all. Either way, the accusation needs a clear answer, and it needs one before the hearing.

## Don't wait on a warrant

If you learn there is a violation warrant or a hearing date, call before you do anything else. The time between the notice and the hearing is your time to prepare. Use it.

Until your lawyer tells you otherwise, keep reporting and keep following every condition you were given. Showing up still counts.

## What to gather before you call

- **Your probation order.** The judgment and any list of rules you signed.
- **The violation papers.** The warrant, report, notice or hearing date.
- **Proof of what you did do.** Payment receipts, class or treatment attendance records, test results, appointment cards and texts with your officer.
- **Your reasons.** If you missed something, write down why: a work shift, a hospital visit, a car that broke down, a sick child. Bring proof if you have it.
- **A timeline.** Dates of each meeting, test, payment and court date since your probation began.
