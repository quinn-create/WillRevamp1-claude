## Our target

The site aims to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at Level AA. These are the international guidelines for making web content usable by people with disabilities.

We treat that as a working goal, not a finished claim. The site is tested before launch, and the same tests are there to run again whenever a page changes.

[cards: 3]
## What we did

### Built to be read by anything
- Plain, structured pages with one main heading and headings in order
- Named page regions, so screen readers can jump to the menu, the content or the footer
- A "Skip to content" link at the top of every page
- Text descriptions for meaningful images; decorative images are hidden from screen readers
- Each page declares its language, English or Spanish, so screen readers pronounce it correctly

### Usable without a mouse
- Every link, button and menu works with a keyboard alone
- A clear outline shows where you are as you tab through a page
- The mobile call bar is sized for easy tapping and never covers the item you are on
- Tap targets are large enough for a thumb

### Easy to see and read
- Text and buttons meet AA color-contrast levels, checked against the site's color palette
- Text can be enlarged to 200% without losing content
- Pages reflow on a narrow phone screen without sideways scrolling
- No autoplaying slideshows, and animation is turned off when your device asks for reduced motion
- Short sentences and plain words, with legal terms explained

## Forms and phone links

Every form field has a visible label, not just placeholder text. Errors are explained in words, not only by color, and a summary lists them at the top.

Every phone number on the site is a link that dials the office when tapped. {fact:F015}

## How we test

- Automated checks with axe on every English and Spanish page, with no serious or critical issues allowed
- Keyboard-only walk-throughs of the menu, the contact page and the cookie settings
- Checks at phone, tablet and desktop widths
- Lighthouse accessibility audits as a second opinion

## Known limitations

We know about these and are working within them:

- **Certificate scans.** The About page shows images of two course-completion certificates. {fact:F064,F065} Each image has a text description, but the scanned documents themselves are pictures, not text.
- **Testimonials appear exactly as published.** That includes one review with a missing word and one that stops mid-sentence. {fact:F133,F134} Screen readers will read them as published.
- **Other websites.** Links to other websites, such as Google Maps and the newspaper story on In the News, open sites we do not control. Their accessibility is up to them.
- **Spam check.** If the contact form uses Cloudflare Turnstile, it may sometimes ask you to complete a check. If it blocks you, call or email instead.
- **Contact form not yet on.** Until the form is switched on, the contact page offers the phone and email instead. Both work with any device.

## Report a barrier

If you can't use part of the site, please tell us. It helps to mention:

- The page address, or what you were trying to find
- What happened, and what you expected to happen
- Your device, browser and any assistive technology you use
