## Take a breath. Then take these steps.

DCS contact is frightening, and it is easy to say or sign something in a panic. Slow down and do these things first.

- **Keep every paper.** Business cards, letters, notices and anything you were asked to sign.
- **Write it down today.** Dates, names, what was asked and what you said, while it is fresh.
- **Keep every date.** Note each appointment, interview and court date, and show up for it.
- **Read before you sign.** If you are handed a plan or agreement, make sure you understand it. Ask for a copy.
- **Talk to a lawyer before the interview.** Will helps parents prepare for DCS interviews. {fact:F236,F242}

## How a DCS case can move

1. **First contact.** A caseworker reaches out about a report. Will helps you respond and get ready for the interview. {fact:F236,F242}
2. **The investigation.** DCS looks into the report. {fact:F235} Keep your own record of every visit and conversation.
3. **Court, if it goes there.** Removal hearings and dependency and neglect cases are heard in court, and Will represents you there. {fact:F237,F238}
4. **The plan.** If a child is out of the home, a permanency plan sets out what has to happen next. {fact:F240} Will works toward reunification whenever possible. {fact:F242}
5. **Reviews.** The court reviews the child's placement as the case goes on. {fact:F241}
