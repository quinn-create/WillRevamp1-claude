## How the site is built

The site is a set of plain, pre-built pages. It has no logins, no accounts, no comments and no shopping cart.

It is hosted on Cloudflare Pages. When you open a page, Cloudflare receives the technical details every web request carries: your IP address, your browser and device type, the page you asked for and the time. Cloudflare uses them to deliver the page and to protect the site from attacks. We do not use them to identify you.

The site's fonts and images are served from the site itself, not from outside font or image services.

## The contact form

[vendor: web3forms]
The contact form asks for your name and phone number. Email and a short message are optional.

When you press Send, your entry goes through Web3Forms, a form-delivery service, to the office inbox at [inbox@willfraleylaw.com](mailto:inbox@willfraleylaw.com). {fact:F019,F020} Web3Forms receives what you typed plus basic technical data, such as your IP address, so it can deliver the message and block abuse. Its own privacy policy is at [web3forms.com/privacy](https://web3forms.com/privacy).

We use what you send for one purpose: to reply to you about your request. Sending the form does not sign you up for a mailing list or for text messages.

Do not put confidential information in the form or in a first email. Sending a message does not create an attorney-client relationship. {fact:F112} Save the details for your consultation.

### Cloudflare Turnstile
[vendor: turnstile]
To keep spam out, the form may run Cloudflare Turnstile, a check that tells people from bots. It looks at signals from your browser, such as how the page was loaded, and usually needs nothing from you. It runs only on pages that show the form. Cloudflare's privacy policy is at [cloudflare.com/privacypolicy](https://www.cloudflare.com/privacypolicy/).

## Analytics and marketing tools

The site is built so that analytics and marketing tools are off by default. The firm decides whether to turn any of them on. Even then, none of them loads until you say yes on the consent banner or on [Cookie Settings](/cookie-settings/).

[vendor-off: tracking]
Right now, no analytics or marketing tools are turned on. No tracking cookies are set, whatever you choose.

If the firm turns a tool on later, it will be listed below, and the site will ask for your consent again.

### Google Analytics 4
[vendor: ga4]
Category: Analytics. Google Analytics counts visits and shows which pages people read, how they arrived and roughly where they are (city or region), along with device and browser type. It sets cookies such as `_ga`. Google's privacy policy is at [policies.google.com/privacy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

### Microsoft Clarity
[vendor: clarity]
Category: Analytics. Clarity shows how visitors use a page, such as clicks, scrolling and where people stop reading, and can build a replay of a visit. It is set to hide what you type into forms. It sets cookies such as `_clck` and `_clsk`. Microsoft's privacy statement is at [privacy.microsoft.com](https://privacy.microsoft.com/privacystatement).

### Meta Pixel
[vendor: meta]
Category: Marketing. The Meta Pixel tells Meta (Facebook and Instagram) that you visited the site, so the firm can measure its ads and show ads to people who have visited. Meta may connect this to a Meta account you are logged into. It sets cookies such as `_fbp`. Meta's privacy policy is at [facebook.com/privacy/policy](https://www.facebook.com/privacy/policy/).

### TikTok Pixel
[vendor: tiktok]
Category: Marketing. The TikTok Pixel tells TikTok that you visited the site, so the firm can measure its ads and show ads to people who have visited. TikTok may connect this to a TikTok account. It sets cookies such as `_ttp`. TikTok's privacy policy is at [tiktok.com/legal/privacy-policy-us](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/privacy-policy-us).

### What these tools never receive

Marketing and analytics tools never receive what you type into the contact form. If you say no, or never answer, they do not load at all.

## How your choice is stored

Your answer is saved on your own device in two places: a first-party cookie named `consent` and a matching entry in your browser's local storage.

They hold four things: your answer for analytics, your answer for marketing, the version of this policy you answered, and the date and time you chose. Nothing about your choice is sent to a server.

If this policy changes in a way that affects your choices, the version number changes and the site asks you again. Clearing your browser data also clears your choice.

## Global Privacy Control

If your browser sends a Global Privacy Control (GPC) signal, the site treats it as a "no" to analytics and marketing. Those tools do not load while the signal is on, and the switches on [Cookie Settings](/cookie-settings/) show as off.

We share it only with the services named on this page, and only so they can do the job described here. We may also disclose it when the law requires it.
