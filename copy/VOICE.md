# VOICE — Will Fraley, Attorney at Law

Owner: A16 (copy director). Binds every EN and ES page. Inputs: `plan/REVAMP-BRIEF.md` §3, `plan/rules/law-firm.md`,
`inventory/CONFLICTS.md`. Facts are frozen; this guide governs only how we say them.

The reader is usually on a phone, often late at night or the morning after an arrest, a DCS visit or a court
paper. They are scared and short on attention. Every page answers three things before anything else: what to
do now, how to reach Will Fraley, and whether it costs anything to start.

---

## 1. Five voice traits

| # | Trait | What it sounds like | What it never sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Steady** | A calm lawyer across a desk in Murfreesboro. Short sentences, low pulse. | An ad. Exclamation points. Fear-selling. |
| 2 | **Plain** | Everyday words. "Charge", "court date", "papers", "parenting time". Legal terms explained once, in passing. | Latin, statute numbers, "heretofore", "in the event that". |
| 3 | **Direct** | Second person, active voice, answer first. "You've been charged. Here's what to do today." | "Individuals who have been charged may wish to consider…" |
| 4 | **Honest** | Singular and exact: "Will Fraley", "Will", "our office". Only ledgered facts. Seriousness comes from specifics. | "Our attorneys", "our legal team", "best", "top", "premier", year counts, promises. |
| 5 | **Local** | Murfreesboro, Rutherford County, the courthouse square, the people who live here. Spanish spoken. | Generic "nationwide" or "big-city" language; claims about courts or counties the ledger doesn't name. |

## 3. Sentence-length targets

- **Average:** 12–16 words per sentence on every page. Hero and CTA lines: 4–12 words.
- **Ceiling:** no sentence over 25 words. If a sentence needs a comma list longer than five items, make it a
  bullet list.
- **Paragraphs:** 1–3 sentences, never more than 4 lines on a 390 px phone.
- **Rhythm:** follow a long sentence with a short one. Short sentences carry the decisions ("Call first.
  Then write it down.").
- **Reading grade:** 8 on home, hubs and contact; 9–10 at most on legal-process pages (DUI, divorce, DCS,
  custody). Test every page; if it reads above 10, cut clauses before cutting facts.
- **Headings:** plain statements or the reader's own question. Under eight words. No puns.

## 4. How we talk about outcomes

Tennessee RPC 7.1 forbids misleading statements, and every reader wants to hear a promise. We give them
certainty about the **process**, never about the **result**.

1. **Promise effort and attention, not outcomes.** Say what Will does ("reviews the charge and the evidence",
   "looks for every lawful way to get the charge reduced or dismissed"), never what a judge will do.
2. **Use "can", "may" and "your options", not "will" about results.** "Some charges can be reduced or
   dismissed. Your consultation is where you learn whether yours is one of them."
3. **No numbers, records, win rates or "cases handled".** None is ledgered; none is allowed.
4. **No comparison words:** best, top, leading, premier, #1, most experienced, second to none.
5. **Never** "specialist", "specializes", "expert", "certified" or "guarantee". Use "focuses on", "practices",
   "handles". The two TACDL certificates are *completed training*, shown as documents.
6. **The disclaimer travels with the result.** Any page that mentions a result, settlement, verdict or
   dismissal — including inside a testimonial — carries, on its own line near that mention:
   "Prior results do not guarantee a similar outcome." (ES: "Los resultados anteriores no garantizan un
   resultado similar.")
7. **Testimonials speak for themselves.** Quote them verbatim, typos and truncation included, with the
   published attribution. Never paraphrase them into firm claims.

## 6. Spanish voice

Neutral Latin-American Spanish, "usted", the same grade-8 plainness. Written, not machine-translated. On first
mention a court term carries its English form in parentheses ("plan de crianza (parenting plan)") because the
reader's court papers will be in English. Spanish pages make no claim the English page does not make and cite
only its fact IDs.
