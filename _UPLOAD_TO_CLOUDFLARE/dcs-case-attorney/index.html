Take a breath. Then take these steps.

DCS contact is frightening, and it is easy to say or sign something in a panic. Slow down and do these things first.

  • Keep every paper. Business cards, letters, notices and anything you were asked to sign.
  • Write it down today. Dates, names, what was asked and what you said, while it is fresh.
  • Keep every date. Note each appointment, interview and court date, and show up for it.
  • Read before you sign. If you are handed a plan or agreement, make sure you understand it. Ask for a copy.
  • Talk to a lawyer before the interview. Will helps parents prepare for DCS interviews.

What a DCS case can involve

Not every case goes through every stage. These are the matters Will handles.

  • Investigations and interviews

    A caseworker looks into a report and talks with the family. Will helps you respond appropriately and prepare for the interview.

  • Protective custody and removal hearings

    If a child has been taken out of the home, the court may hold hearings about it. Will represents parents at those hearings.

  • Dependency and neglect cases

    These are the court cases where allegations about a child’s care are decided. Will represents parents and guardians in them.

  • Reunification and permanency plans

    When a child is out of the home, a plan lays out the steps and the goal. Will works toward reunification whenever possible.

  • Foster care placement and court reviews

    The court checks on a child’s placement over time. Will is with you at those reviews.

  • Termination of parental rights

    A case to end a parent’s legal rights is one of the most serious a family can face. Will handles these cases.

When a criminal charge is part of it

Sometimes the same events lead to a DCS case and a criminal charge. Will’s practice covers both criminal court and family court. If you are facing both, say so when you call (615) 410-7290 for a free consultation.

The process

How a DCS case can move

  1. 01

    First contact.

    A caseworker reaches out about a report. Will helps you respond and get ready for the interview.

  2. 02

    The investigation.

    DCS looks into the report. Keep your own record of every visit and conversation.

  3. 03

    Court, if it goes there.

    Removal hearings and dependency and neglect cases are heard in court, and Will represents you there.

  4. 04

    The plan.

    If a child is out of the home, a permanency plan sets out what has to happen next. Will works toward reunification whenever possible.

  5. 05

    Reviews.

    The court reviews the child’s placement as the case goes on.

DCS hasn’t gone to court. Should I still call a lawyer?

You can call at any stage. Will helps parents with DCS investigations and interviews, not only court cases.
