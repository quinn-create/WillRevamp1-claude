An accusation is not the whole story

A theft charge can feel personal in a way other charges don’t. It can sound like a judgment on your character, not just a claim about one afternoon.

It is an accusation, not a conviction. What happened, what you meant and what the evidence shows are all still open questions. Your defense starts with getting them answered.

Theft charges Will Fraley handles

Will helps people accused of theft, whether the charge is a misdemeanor or something more serious. He handles state felony and misdemeanor charges.

  • Theft

    Charges that say you took property or money that belonged to someone else, from a store, an employer or a person.

  • Robbery

    Robbery charges, which usually claim that force or a threat came with a theft. Some robbery charges overlap with violent crimes.

  • Burglary

    Burglary charges, which usually claim that someone went into a building, home or vehicle without permission to commit a crime.

  • White-collar charges

    Theft through paperwork, accounts or a job, such as embezzlement. See fraud defense for the full list.

What to do before your first court date

  • Talk to a lawyer before you talk to anyone else. That includes the store, your employer and the person who made the accusation.
  • Don’t try to fix it on your own. Offers to pay something back or to explain can be misunderstood. Ask Will first.
  • Keep your records. Receipts, bank statements, texts and emails can show what really happened.
  • Write down names. Who was there, who saw what, and who you spoke to afterward.

The process

How a theft case moves

  1. 01

    The charge.

    An arrest or a citation comes with a first court date. Bring every paper you were given.

  2. 02

    Your consultation.

    You give your side. Will explains the charge and your options in plain terms.

  3. 03

    The facts.

    Your defense goes through the reports, any video, the witness statements and the paper trail, and checks whether the story holds together.

  4. 04

    Your options.

    Will looks for every lawful way to get the charges reduced or dismissed. Some cases are resolved without a trial. Others are decided in court.

  5. 05

    Court.

    If your case is tried, Will presents your defense in the courtroom as a dedicated trial attorney.

When the accusation comes from work

Some theft charges begin with an employer: missing cash, a shortfall in the books, or an audit that points at you. These cases can turn on records as much as on witnesses.

If that is your situation, gather what you can lawfully keep, such as your own pay stubs and messages. Don’t take company files. Bring what you have to your consultation, and read the fraud defense page for related charges.

If someone in your family was accused

A parent, spouse or adult child can make the first call. Have their full name ready, plus any citation or court date. Call (615) 410-7290 for a free consultation.

Questions

Theft charge questions

Is a theft charge always a misdemeanor?

No. Will helps people accused of theft whether the charge is a misdemeanor or a more serious offense. How your charge is classed depends on the facts, and your consultation is where you learn how yours is filed.

Do you also handle robbery and burglary?

Yes. Robbery and burglary charges are part of Will’s theft defense work.

I was accused by my employer. Is that theft or fraud?

It can be either, depending on what is claimed. Will handles theft charges and white-collar charges, including embezzlement.

My charge is minor. Will my case still get real attention?

Yes. Will Fraley and his team personally handle each case, no matter how big or small.
