Steady first. Then a plan.

A violent-crime charge can move fast, and it can feel as if everyone has already made up their mind. They haven’t heard your side yet.

You’ve been charged, not convicted. The work of building a real defense starts with the first phone call. Will Fraley, Attorney at Law, has served many people facing serious allegations, from murder to hit-and-runs.

Violent-crime charges Will Fraley handles

Will defends people accused of the violent crimes below.

  • Assault and battery

    Assault and battery charges, from a fight that got out of hand to an accusation you say never happened.

  • Manslaughter

    Manslaughter charges, including charges that follow a fatal car crash. If alcohol is part of the case, see DUI defense as well.

  • Murder or attempted murder

    Murder and attempted murder charges are among the most serious a person can face, and they call for careful preparation from the start.

  • Violent sexual offenses

    Rape and other sex offenses that are charged as violent crimes. See sex crime defense.

  • Domestic assault

    Assault charges that involve a partner, a family member or someone you live with. See domestic assault defense.

What to avoid right now

  • Don’t contact the person who accused you. Not to apologize, not to explain, not through a friend.
  • Follow every condition of your release. If you were given bond papers, read them and keep them.
  • Don’t talk about the case. Not with friends, not with family, not online. Save it for your lawyer.
  • Do write it down. What happened before, during and after, and the names of everyone who was there.

The process

How a violent-crime case moves

  1. 01

    The arrest.

    The case starts with an arrest and a first court date. Keep every paper you are handed.

  2. 02

    Your consultation.

    Call (615) 410-7290 for a free consultation. You tell Will your side, and you learn what the charge means and what your options are.

  3. 03

    The investigation.

    Your defense goes through the police reports, the witness statements and the physical evidence. It looks at the whole timeline, not just the moment the police describe.

  4. 04

    The witnesses.

    Violent-crime cases can come down to what witnesses say. Will has completed advanced cross-examination training with the Tennessee Association of Criminal Defense Lawyers.

  5. 05

    Your options.

    Will looks for every lawful way to get the charges reduced or dismissed. If your case goes to trial, you have a dedicated trial attorney beside you.

Questions

Violent-crime questions

Do you take murder cases?

Yes. Murder and attempted murder charges are among the violent crimes Will Fraley defends.

The other person started it. What should I do?

Don’t reach out to them, and don’t argue it out online. Write down exactly what happened and who saw it, and bring that to your consultation.

Will my case go to trial?

Some cases end in an agreement and others go to trial. If yours does, you have a dedicated trial attorney beside you.

Who will actually work on my case?

Will Fraley does, with his team. They personally handle criminal cases, no matter how big or small.
