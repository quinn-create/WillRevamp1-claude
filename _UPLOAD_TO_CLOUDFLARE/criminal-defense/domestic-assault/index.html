One charge, many questions

Where can you stay tonight? When can you see your children? What are you allowed to say to the person you live with? A domestic assault charge can raise all of these at once.

You have been charged, not convicted. Start with the paper in your hand, and with one call.

Your bond conditions, in plain terms

If you were released on bond, your release papers should list any conditions you must follow. Read every line.

  • Follow each condition exactly. Even if the other person calls, texts or invites you back.
  • Don’t work around a condition. If it keeps you from your home or your children, bring the papers to your consultation and ask what can be done the right way.
  • Keep the papers with you. Bond papers, any order you were served and your court date.
  • Write it down now. What happened, in order, and who saw or heard any of it.

What “domestic” can mean

Many people think domestic assault only means spouses or partners. It is not limited to romantic relationships: it can also apply to family members and to people who live together, including roommates.

When the people involved don’t have that kind of relationship, the accusation may be charged as another assault offense instead, such as assault or aggravated assault. Will defends those charges too, along with assault and battery.

Questions

Domestic assault questions

The other person doesn’t want to go forward. Is the case over?

Don’t assume it is, and don’t act on it. Keep following your bond conditions, and ask Will at your consultation what it means for your case.

We’re roommates, not a couple. Can it still be domestic assault?

It can. Domestic assault is not limited to couples: people who live together, including roommates, can be covered.

Will this affect my custody case?

It can come up there, so ask about both at once. Will handles criminal defense and family law.
