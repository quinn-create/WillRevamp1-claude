Quiet, careful and early

An accusation like this can reach your work, your family and your name long before a court date. The first job is to keep it from spreading, and to keep your own words out of it until you have a lawyer.

You have been accused, not convicted. The stakes are real, though. Many people convicted of a sex crime must also register as a sex offender. That is why these cases call for careful work from the very first day.

Sex crime charges Will Fraley handles

Will Fraley defends people accused of the offenses below.

  • Rape

    Rape accusations. Rape and other sex crimes are often considered violent offenses. Write down your own timeline now, while the details are fresh.

  • Sexual battery

    Sexual battery charges. Save every message between you and the other person, exactly as it is.

  • Child molestation

    Accusations of child molestation. If the Department of Children’s Services has also contacted your family, tell Will at your consultation. Will also represents parents and guardians in DCS cases.

  • Prostitution

    Prostitution charges. A charge that seems minor still deserves a real answer in court.

What not to do

Most of what protects you this week is restraint.

  • Don’t contact the person who accused you. Not by text, not through a friend, not to “clear the air.”
  • Don’t post, and don’t delete. Stay off social media about the case. Leave messages, photos and accounts as they are.
  • Don’t agree to “just talk.” If a detective or investigator asks you to come in, call a lawyer before you go.
  • Don’t tell more people than you must. Until you have talked to a lawyer, keep the circle small.

Keeping it private

Call from a phone and a place where you can speak freely. Who you tell about the accusation is your decision.

If you use the contact form, keep the message short. Save the details for the conversation itself.
