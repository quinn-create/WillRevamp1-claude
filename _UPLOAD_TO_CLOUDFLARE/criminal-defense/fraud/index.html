A paper case still has a person at the center

Fraud cases are built from records: bank statements, emails, invoices, tax filings. Stacked together, they can look like the whole story. They may not be.

You have been charged, not convicted. Many fraud charges are felonies, so take this one seriously from the first day. Your job right now is simple: protect your records, and stop explaining yourself to anyone but your lawyer.

Fraud and white-collar charges Will Fraley handles

Will defends people charged with fraud and white-collar crimes, including the charges below.

  • Embezzlement

    Embezzlement charges. Write down what your duties were and who else could reach the accounts or the money.

  • Forgery

    Charges that documents were falsified. Write down what each document in question is, and who holds the original.

  • Identity theft and card fraud

    Accusations of using another person’s personal information, or their credit or debit card.

  • Insurance fraud

    Charges that an insurance claim or policy was used dishonestly to gain money.

  • Wire, mail and tax fraud

    Fraud alleged to have been committed through emails, calls or texts, through the mail, or through false statements on a tax return.

  • Money laundering, counterfeiting and bribery

    These are white-collar crimes too, and Will defends white-collar charges.

The records to gather

Bring copies to your consultation and keep the originals somewhere safe.

  • Your charging papers. The warrant, summons or citation, your bond papers and any court notice.
  • Letters and notices. Anything from a bank, an insurer, an employer, a tax office or an investigator.
  • Your own financial records. Bank and card statements, receipts, invoices and contracts for the period in question.
  • Messages. Emails and texts tied to the accusation. Don’t delete or edit any of them.
  • A list of names. Who handled the money or the paperwork, and who can explain how things really worked.

Keep only what is yours to keep. If a record belongs to a business or someone else, write down where it is instead.

    Your options.

When the accuser is someone you know

Sometimes the accusation comes from a relative, a former business partner or an employer you trusted. Feelings run high on both sides.

It is tempting to call them and sort it out yourself. Don’t. Let your lawyer handle every contact about the case, so you don’t hand anyone words that could be used against you.

Fraud questions

Is fraud a felony?

Many fraud charges are. How yours is charged depends on the facts, and you can talk it through at your consultation.

Should I offer to pay the money back?

Not before you talk to a lawyer. An offer made the wrong way can read like an admission.
