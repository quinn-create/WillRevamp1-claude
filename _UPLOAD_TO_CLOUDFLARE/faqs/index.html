Getting started

Is the first consultation really free?

Yes. Call (615) 410-7290 for a free consultation. It is a chance to talk through your situation and your legal options before you decide anything.

Who will handle my case?

On a criminal matter, Will Fraley and his team personally handle your case, no matter how big or small it may be. When you reach out, the office sets up your initial consultation.

Questions

Criminal charges

You’ve been charged, not convicted. These are the questions people ask first.

Do I really need a lawyer?

A criminal charge is serious, and choices made early in a case can matter later. A lawyer can go through the charge and the evidence with you before your first court date. Will looks for every lawful way to get the charges reduced or dismissed. No lawyer can promise you a particular result.

What kinds of criminal cases does Will Fraley handle?

State felony and misdemeanor charges. That includes DUI, drug crimes, theft, violent crimes, sex crimes, fraud, probation violations and domestic assault. The criminal defense page has a page for each.

If I’m convicted, what punishment could I face?

That depends on the charge, the facts and your record. Nobody can answer it honestly before looking at your case. Your consultation is where you learn what you may be facing and what your options are.

I’ve been charged with drug possession. Will I go to jail?

A charge is not a conviction, and the answer depends on the details of your case. Will handles possession, paraphernalia and distribution charges. Bring every paper you were given to your consultation. More on the drug crimes page.

Could my case end up in federal court?

Some drug cases can. Drug trafficking across state or international borders can become a federal offense. If a federal agency is involved in your case, say so when you call.

What if I’m accused of theft?

Theft accusations range from misdemeanors to more serious charges. Will handles theft, robbery and burglary charges. How your charge is graded is a question to bring to your consultation. More on the theft page.

Questions

DUI stops and charges

What DUI-related charges does Will handle?

DUI first and second offense, felony DUI and underage DUI. He also handles driving while suspended, reckless driving and vehicular homicide.

What penalties can a DUI carry?

Penalties can include fines, license suspension, probation, mandatory DUI classes and jail. What applies in a given case can turn on the blood alcohol level and any prior DUIs. The DUI page explains the process.

An officer asks if I’ve been drinking. What should I say?

You can politely decline to answer until you have spoken with a lawyer. Stay calm and courteous, and don’t argue at the roadside.

What about roadside tests and breath or blood tests?

Test rules are detailed, and a short summary on a web page could mislead you. After a stop, write down which tests you were asked to take and which you took or declined. Bring those notes to your consultation, where you can get an answer that fits your case.

Family court

Should I take the children with me when I move out?

Talk to a lawyer before you move out, move the children or change their routine. The right first step depends on your family, and early choices can come up later in court. Call (615) 410-7290 for a free consultation.

What is joint custody?

In general terms, joint custody can mean shared time, shared decisions, or both.

  • Joint physical custody: both parents share significant time with the children.
  • Joint legal custody: both parents consult on major decisions about education, religion and health.
  • Full joint custody: both kinds of rights, shared equally.

The terms used in your own case may differ, so ask how they apply to you at your consultation. Will helps clients work out who makes decisions for a child and whether custody is sole or shared.

How does a judge decide custody?

Custody decisions focus on the best interests of the child. Will handles custody matters through cooperative negotiation or by arguing before the court. More on the child custody page.

Do courts still favor mothers?

Mothers were once favored, but custody decisions now focus on the best interests of the child. Will Fraley advocates for parents in family court. The firm has represented fathers who are trying to prove a child is theirs, as well as mothers who are attempting to establish paternity.

What does a divorce have to settle?

A divorce can involve child custody and visitation, child support, property division and spousal support. Will can guide you through these disputes, including child custody negotiations. More on the divorce page.

Can a parenting plan be changed later?

Sometimes. Reasons a plan may be changed include a parent moving away, a change in either parent’s finances, or parents who no longer agree. Will can help you propose a change or answer one your ex has proposed. See parenting plan modifications.

DCS contacted me. What now?

Will represents parents and guardians in DCS cases in Murfreesboro and Rutherford County. That includes DCS investigations and interviews. If you can, call before the interview. More on the DCS cases page.

Questions

Injury claims

I was hurt in an accident. What should I do first?

Get medical care first. Then talk to a lawyer before you give a statement to the other side’s insurance company or lawyer. Keep every bill, photo and letter.

How do I know if I have a claim?

In general terms, an injury claim rests on four questions.

  • Did the other person owe you a duty of care?
  • Did they breach that duty?
  • Were you harmed?
  • Did their actions cause the harm?

If you’re not sure, ask. Call (615) 410-7290 for a free consultation.

What can I be compensated for?

Damages can include medical bills, property damage, lost wages, pain and suffering, permanent scars, emotional distress and disability. What a claim may be worth depends on its facts.

What if I was partly to blame?

Being partly at fault does not always end a claim. How fault is shared depends on what happened, so tell the whole story at your consultation.

Do I have to take the other person to court?

Often not. Many personal injury cases are settled directly with the at-fault party’s insurance company. Some do go to court, and Will handles both negotiation and litigation.

My car may be totaled. What happens now?

Generally, a car is considered totaled when repairs would cost more than the car is worth. Keep your repair estimates and any listings for similar cars. They help when you talk about value.

Who pays my medical bills in the meantime?

That depends on the insurance policies involved and the facts of the crash. Bring your insurance cards, policy papers and every letter from an insurer to your consultation. More on the personal injury page.
