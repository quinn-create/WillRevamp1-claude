Where you may be right now

Maybe you are the one deciding to file. Maybe your spouse already has, and the papers just arrived. Either way, the questions come fast: where the children will live, how the bills get paid, who keeps the house.

You don’t have to answer them all today. Your first conversation is where you lay out what is happening and discuss your legal options. From there, you can decide on a next step with a clearer picture.

What a divorce case can cover

The disputes in a divorce can include custody and visitation, child support, property division and spousal support. Will handles each of them.

  • Child support

    If you have children together, child support will likely need to be addressed. Child support is one of the divorce issues Will negotiates, so you don’t have to settle every detail face to face with your spouse.

  • Custody and parenting time

    Custody of minor children can be one of the hardest issues in a divorce. Will helps clients present a clear, well-organized request for custody that takes the legal factors into account.

    Child custody

  • Spousal support

    Spousal support, also called alimony or spousal maintenance, is a common point of dispute. Will helps clients who are filing for spousal maintenance pursue it.

  • Division of property

    Who keeps what can be hard to agree on. In Tennessee divorce cases, a judge divides property under a principle called “equitable distribution,” and fault is not a consideration. Will helps you present your side of the division clearly.

  • Paternity during a divorce

    If parenthood needs to be proven while the divorce is pending, Will can advise you and handle the paperwork.

    Paternity

  • A child adopted during the marriage

    If you adopted a child during your marriage, custody still has to be decided, and Will can review the adoption paperwork with you and talk through what to file in court.

What to gather before your consultation

You don’t need a perfect file. Bring what you can find:

  • Any court papers you have filed or been handed.
  • A rough list of what you own and what you owe, together and separately.
  • Recent pay stubs or tax returns, yours and your spouse’s if you have them.
  • Your children’s routine: school, activities, who usually handles what.
  • Your questions, written down, so nothing gets lost in the moment.

The process

How a divorce usually moves

  1. 01

    The consultation.

    You explain your situation and what matters most to you. Call (615) 410-7290 for a free initial consultation.

  2. 02

    Filing.

    A divorce case begins with a petition, and Will helps clients petition for divorce.

  3. 03

    Naming the issues.

Questions

Questions about divorce

My spouse already filed. What should I do?

Call soon, and bring the papers you received to your consultation so you can discuss your legal options. Call (615) 410-7290 for a free initial consultation.

How is property divided in Tennessee?

In Tennessee divorce cases, a judge divides property under a principle called “equitable distribution,” and fault is not a consideration. How that applies to your home, accounts and debts depends on your facts.

Will our divorce go to trial?

Not necessarily. Spouses on amicable terms may be able to settle some disputes by agreement or through mediation. When they can’t, Will argues your side before the court.

Do you handle alimony?

Yes. Spousal support, or alimony, is one of the divorce issues Will handles.
