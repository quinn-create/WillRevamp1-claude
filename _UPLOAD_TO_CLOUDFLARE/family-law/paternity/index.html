Why paternity comes first

Paternity often matters in custody and child support cases. If there is a question about who the father is, the court will require proof before it moves ahead on either one.

That puts paternity at the front of the line: a father who wants time with his child may have to settle it first, and so may a mother who is asking the father to help support their child.

Where you might be standing

  • You believe the child is yours. You and the mother are not together, and she denies it or isn’t sure. The firm has represented fathers trying to prove a child is theirs.
  • You are raising the child. You need the father named so support and parenting time can be addressed. The firm has represented mothers working to establish paternity.
  • You are being named, and you don’t think it’s you. A paternity action is also where a claim that isn’t true gets answered.
  • You are in the middle of a divorce. If parenthood becomes a question in the case, Will can advise you and handle the paperwork filed to prove it.

What to bring to your consultation

Bring what you have. Missing papers won’t stop the conversation.

  • The child’s birth certificate, if you have a copy.
  • Any court papers you have filed or been served with.
  • Messages with the other parent about the pregnancy, the child or money.
  • A record of any support you have paid or received, and when.
  • A simple calendar of the time you have spent with the child.

Questions

Questions about paternity

I’m not with the mother. Can I still establish paternity?

A paternity action is how a father asks the court to establish that a child is his. The firm has represented fathers trying to prove a child is theirs.

Someone says I’m the father, and I don’t think I am. What can I do?

A paternity action can also answer a claim that isn’t true. Talk to a lawyer before you sign anything that names you as the father.

Does paternity affect custody and child support?

Often, yes. When the father’s identity is in question, the court requires proof before it moves forward on custody or support.

Will I need a DNA test?

Will recommends DNA testing as the gold standard for this kind of question. At the consultation, you can talk through how testing fits your case.
