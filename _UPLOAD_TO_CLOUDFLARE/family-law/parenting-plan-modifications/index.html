A plan is not set in stone

Children grow. Jobs change. Parents move, or simply stop seeing eye to eye. A schedule built for a toddler may not fit a teenager.

A parenting plan can be modified to account for changes in the parents’ or the children’s circumstances. Will handles these cases, starting with the filing for modification of the existing plan.

Common reasons to revisit a plan

The old plan may need to change when one of these happens.

  • A parent is moving

    When one parent plans on moving away, the current schedule may no longer work. The plan may need to catch up with the new distance.

  • Money has changed

    A change in either parent’s financial situation, such as a new job or a lost one, can be a reason to revisit the plan. Child support is one of the terms a parenting plan can include.

  • You no longer agree

    Sometimes terms that once worked no longer do. When one parent, or both, no longer agree with the existing terms, a modification is one way forward.

What a plan can cover

A change might touch one term or several. A parenting plan can include details about:

  • Child support
  • The visitation schedule
  • Insurance
  • Education
  • Religious upbringing

Asking for a change, or answering one

If you want the change, Will can propose it and take it through the legal process.

If your former spouse wants it and you don’t, Will can help you work out what to do next.

Either way, decisions about parenting plans are almost always made based on the best interests of the children. So expect the focus to be on how the change affects the children, not only on whether a parent agrees with it.

What to bring to your consultation

  • Your current parenting plan and any court order that goes with it.
  • A short timeline of what has changed, and when.
  • Papers that show the change, such as a new lease, a job offer or pay records.
  • Your child’s current schedule: school, activities and care.
  • Any written proposal the other parent has sent you.

Questions

Questions about changing a parenting plan

Can a parenting plan be changed after the divorce is final?

Yes. A parenting plan can be modified to account for changes in the family’s circumstances. Will handles requests for modification of an existing plan.

What are common reasons for a change?

A parent planning to move, a change in either parent’s finances, or parents who no longer agree with the existing terms.

My ex wants to change our plan, and I don’t agree. What now?

Will can help you figure out what to do next. Expect the focus to be on the children, since parenting-plan decisions are almost always made based on their best interests.

Do both parents have to agree to a change?

Not necessarily. If your former spouse proposes a change you don’t agree with, Will can help you respond, and a disputed change can be argued before the court.
