# Direction A: "Counsel"

**The idea:** the site reads like a well-set legal brief. Calm paper, dark ink and one clear blue let the
words and the phone number carry the authority.

## Why it fits Will Fraley and his clients

Most visitors are on a phone, frightened, and short on time (`copy/VOICE.md`). They need composure, not noise.
The brand audit (`audit/A07.md`) found that the current site spreads itself across five typefaces, stock
handcuffs and computer screenshots. Its real assets are the logo blue and two honest photos of Will. Counsel
removes everything else. The site audit (`audit/AUDIT.md`) ranks a slow, hard-to-call site as the first two
problems. This direction uses almost no imagery, so it stays fast, and the phone button is the only solid
color block on most screens.

## Palette

- **Paper** #F7F6F2 for the page, **Linen** #EFEDE7 for alternate sections, **Card** #FCFBF8
- **Ink** #16202C for body text, headings and the footer band
- **Slate** #5A6472 for muted text
- **Logo Blue** #447CB7 for large type, rules and the wordmark only
- **Counsel Blue** #2E5F96 for links and the phone button
- **Navy** #1D478A for link hover
- **Courthouse Brass** #8C6A2F, a quiet accent kept to the focus ring and step numbers

All 45 declared pairs pass WCAG AA. Body text is 15.2:1 and the button label is 6.6:1.

## Type

**Newsreader** (display) and **Public Sans** (text). Both are variable, self-hosted, and have full Spanish
accents. Newsreader is a newspaper serif with optical sizes, so large headings feel engraved and composed.
Public Sans was drawn for U.S. government sites and is plain, civic and very legible on small screens.

## The logo

The redrawn blue wordmark always sits on paper, where it reads cleanly. The tagline is dropped below 200 px
wide. The dark footer sets the firm name in Newsreader rather than recoloring the logo.

## Best at

It builds trust through restraint. It makes a small-town practice look as composed as a downtown firm, and its
pages load fast.

## Risks

The design is quiet, and quiet can read as cold or generic. Warmth has to come from Will's real portrait,
plain-spoken copy and the brass details. If the photo is weak or the copy turns stiff, the page loses its
warmth. The phone button must stay the boldest object on every screen.
